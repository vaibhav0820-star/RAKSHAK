## Not breathing or unresponsive (CPR)
tags: cpr, unconscious, collapsed, no pulse, not breathing, cardiac arrest, unresponsive, heart stopped, not waking
1. Check the scene is safe. Shout and tap the shoulders. No response: shout for help, call 112 and send someone for an AED.
2. Tilt the head back and lift the chin. Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is not normal breathing.
3. Not breathing normally: push hard and fast on the centre of the chest, about 5-6 cm deep, 100-120 times a minute. Let the chest rise fully between pushes.
4. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions. If not, keep doing compressions only.
5. Use an AED as soon as it arrives and follow its voice prompts. Do not stop until help takes over or the person breathes normally.
6. Late pregnancy: start CPR at once. With a second helper, gently push the belly toward her left side.
Call 112 if: always.

## Severe bleeding
tags: heavy bleeding, blood loss, wound, cut, haemorrhage, bleeding won't stop, injury
1. Call 112. Use gloves or a clean plastic bag on your hands if possible.
2. Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth. Keep pressing; do not lift to check.
3. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top. Raise the limb if a fracture is not suspected.
4. Bandage firmly once bleeding slows. Only if a limb is bleeding dangerously and pressure fails, tie a wide strip tightly above the wound and note the time.
5. Lay the person down, keep them warm, give nothing to eat or drink. Do not pull out objects stuck in a wound; press around them.

## Choking
tags: choke, cannot breathe, food stuck, airway blocked, swallowed
1. If they can cough or speak, encourage coughing and stay close.
2. If they cannot breathe, speak or cough: lean them forward and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades.
3. Then up to 5 abdominal thrusts: fist above the navel, pull sharply inward and upward. In late pregnancy or for a very large person, give chest thrusts at the centre of the chest instead.
4. Alternate back blows and thrusts. Call 112 if it does not clear. If they become unresponsive, start CPR.
5. Infant under 1 year: 5 back blows with the head lower than the body along your forearm, then 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. No abdominal thrusts.

## Chest pain or suspected heart attack
tags: heart attack, chest pressure, tight chest, pain in left arm, jaw pain, sweating, breathless, angina
1. Call 112. Stop all activity and sit the person half upright with knees bent.
2. Loosen tight clothing and keep them calm. Do not let them walk or drive.
3. If they are not allergic and a doctor has not told them to avoid it, they may chew one 300 mg aspirin.
4. Women often have less typical signs: breathlessness, nausea, back or jaw pain, unusual tiredness. Take them seriously.
5. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, start CPR.

## Stroke
tags: stroke, face drooping, slurred speech, arm weakness, sudden numbness, confusion, brain attack
1. Think FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech slurred, Time to call 112.
2. Note the time the symptoms started and tell the responders.
3. Keep them sitting or lying with the head slightly raised. Give nothing to eat or drink and no aspirin.
4. Sudden severe headache, loss of vision or confusion also need urgent care.

## Seizure or fits
tags: seizure, convulsion, fit, epilepsy, shaking, eclampsia
1. Note the time. Clear hard objects away and cushion the head.
2. Do not hold them down or put anything in their mouth.
3. When the shaking stops, turn them onto their side and check breathing. Stay until they are fully awake.
4. Call 112 if it lasts more than 5 minutes, repeats, it is their first seizure, they are injured, or they are pregnant.

## Burns
tags: burn, scald, fire injury, hot oil, hot water, blister
1. Move away from the heat. Remove rings and loose clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
2. Cool with clean running water for 20 minutes. No ice, butter, toothpaste or oil.
3. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth. Do not burst blisters.
4. Go to hospital or call 112 for burns larger than the person's palm, on the face, hands, genitals or joints, deep, chemical or electrical burns, or in children and older people.
5. Clothes on fire: stop, drop and roll.

## Fracture or injury
tags: fracture, broken bone, sprain, swelling, fall, spine injury, neck injury
1. Do not move them unless in danger. Keep the injured part still.
2. Do not straighten the limb. Support it as found with rolled clothes or a padded splint covering the joints above and below.
3. Suspected neck or back injury: keep head and body still and call 112.
4. If fingers or toes turn pale, blue or numb, loosen the wrap. A cold pack wrapped in cloth reduces swelling.

## Fainting or dizziness
tags: faint, dizzy, blackout, light headed, collapse
1. Lay the person flat and raise the legs about 30 cm. Loosen tight clothing and give fresh air.
2. If pregnant, lay her on her left side instead of flat on her back.
3. When awake and able to swallow, give sips of water or something sweet.
4. Call 112 if they do not wake within a minute, are injured, have chest pain, or are pregnant with bleeding or severe pain.

## Heat exhaustion and heatstroke
tags: heatwave, sunstroke, hot, dehydration, confusion, hot dry skin, extreme heat
1. Move to shade or a cool room. Remove extra clothing.
2. Cool quickly: wet the skin, fan, and place cold packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
3. If awake, give small sips of cool water or ORS.
4. Confusion, fits, fainting or hot dry skin means heatstroke: call 112 and keep cooling until help arrives.
5. Pregnant women, older people and children overheat faster.

## Low blood sugar (diabetes)
tags: diabetes, hypoglycaemia, low sugar, shaky, sweating, insulin
1. Signs: shaking, sweating, hunger, confusion, irritability.
2. If awake and able to swallow, give about 15 g of fast sugar, such as 3 teaspoons of sugar in water or a glass of juice. Recheck after 15 minutes, repeat if still low, then give a snack.
3. If unconscious or unable to swallow, put nothing in the mouth, place them on their side and call 112.

## Asthma or severe breathlessness
tags: asthma, wheeze, short of breath, inhaler, difficulty breathing, breathless
1. Sit upright and stay calm. Loosen clothing and move away from smoke or dust.
2. Use the reliever inhaler (usually blue) as prescribed, with a spacer if available.
3. If there is no improvement, lips turn blue, or they cannot speak in full sentences, call 112 and keep using the reliever as prescribed.

## Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
tags: allergy, anaphylaxis, swelling throat, hives, sting, adrenaline, epipen, food allergy
1. Signs: swelling of lips, face or throat, wheeze, trouble breathing, widespread hives or dizziness after a sting, food or medicine.
2. Call 112. If they have an adrenaline auto-injector, use it at once on the outer thigh and note the time.
3. Lay them flat with legs raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up. Do not stand them up suddenly.
4. If there is no improvement after 5 to 15 minutes and a second auto-injector is available, use it.

## Snake or animal bite
tags: snake, snakebite, dog bite, rabies, venom, animal bite
1. Move away. Do not catch or kill the animal. Keep the person calm and still.
2. Keep the bitten limb at heart level and remove rings. Wrap loosely and immobilise with a splint.
3. Do not cut, suck, burn or tie tight bands. Do not give alcohol.
4. Go to a hospital with antivenom or rabies treatment at once and call 108 or 112.
5. Dog or animal bite: wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes and seek rabies vaccination.

## Poisoning or swallowed chemicals
tags: poison, swallowed, chemical, overdose, pesticide, kerosene
1. Call 112 and follow their instructions. Keep the container or label to show the doctor.
2. Do not make the person vomit. Remove contaminated clothing.
3. Chemical on skin or eyes: rinse with running water for 15 to 20 minutes.
4. If unconscious, place on their side and check breathing.

## Electric shock
tags: electric shock, electrocution, fallen wire, live wire, current
1. Do not touch the person until the power is off or you have moved the source away with dry wood or plastic.
2. Call 112. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR. Cover burns loosely.
3. Everyone who has had an electric shock should be checked at a hospital. Stay away from fallen wires, especially in floods and storms.
