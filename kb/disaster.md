## Flood and drowning
tags: flood, water rising, drowning, rescue, rain, waterlogged, flash flood
1. Move to higher ground early. Never walk, swim or drive through moving water. Fast water can knock you down at ankle depth and sweep a car away at about 30 cm.
2. Switch off mains power only if it is safe and dry. Stay away from fallen cables.
3. Rescue: reach with a stick or throw a float. Do not jump in.
4. Pulled out not breathing: call 112 and start CPR; rescue breaths matter in drowning.
5. Drink only safe water. Boil or chlorinate it, and avoid floodwater (disease, snakes).

## Earthquake
tags: earthquake, tremor, aftershock, collapse, building, trapped
1. Drop, cover and hold on under a sturdy table. Stay away from windows. Do not run outside during shaking or use lifts.
2. Outdoors: move to open ground away from buildings and wires.
3. After shaking: expect aftershocks. Check for injuries and gas smell, leave damaged buildings by stairs, and do not light matches.
4. If trapped: cover your mouth, tap on a pipe or wall, use a whistle rather than shouting.

## Cyclone and storm
tags: cyclone, storm, wind, hurricane, evacuate, shelter, warning
1. Follow IMD and state warnings and evacuate early to a cyclone shelter on higher ground. Do not wait until the storm starts.
2. Take your go-bag, medicines, documents, power bank and water.
3. Stay indoors away from windows. The calm eye of a cyclone is followed by strong winds again.
4. Afterwards avoid fallen wires and floodwater.

## Fire and smoke
tags: fire, smoke, burning, house fire, fire escape
1. Alert others and leave now. Do not collect belongings. Call 101 or 112.
2. Stay low under smoke and cover your mouth with a wet cloth. Check doors with the back of your hand; do not open a hot door.
3. Close doors behind you. Never use lifts.
4. Clothes on fire: stop, drop and roll.

## Evacuation go-bag checklist
tags: go bag, emergency kit, pack, what to carry, checklist, documents, prepare, readiness
1. Water (about 3 litres per person per day), dry food, ORS.
2. Medicines for 7 days, prescriptions, inhaler or insulin.
3. ID copies, MCP or health card, emergency contacts written down.
4. Power bank, torch, whistle, charged phone, cash.
5. Sanitary pads or clean cloth, soap, spare clothes, baby supplies.
6. Glasses, hearing aids, mobility aids, spare batteries.
7. Light blanket and plastic bags. Fill in your SafeHaven profile and emergency contacts.

## Evacuating with limited mobility, pregnancy or older age
tags: wheelchair, disability, elderly, pregnant evacuation, priority evacuation, accessible, walking difficulty, old age
1. Do not wait. Ask for evacuation early and send an SOS in SafeHaven with your mobility or pregnancy flags so priority transport can be assigned.
2. Tell responders exactly what you need: wheelchair, oxygen, stairs, hearing or vision support.
3. Choose accessible shelters with ramps, accessible toilets and medical staff.
4. Pregnant: avoid long walks, carry your MCP card, and leave early in late pregnancy.
5. Agree a buddy plan with a neighbour in advance.
6. Helpers: ask before lifting. Move someone by chair carry or blanket drag only if staying is more dangerous.

## Living in a shelter: water, sanitation and health
tags: shelter, camp, relief camp, water, sanitation, toilet, diarrhoea, hygiene, food, breastfeeding
1. Drink safe water: boil for 1 minute or use chlorine tablets. Wash hands with soap before eating and after the toilet.
2. Use lit toilets in pairs and report broken locks or lights.
3. Keep food covered. For diarrhoea give ORS and fluids; get help quickly for blood in stool, high fever, very dry mouth or very little urine.
4. Pregnant, breastfeeding and new mothers can ask for priority space, food and a private corner.

## What to tell the emergency dispatcher
tags: call 112, 108, 102, 101, 100, ambulance, dispatcher, emergency number, help line
1. Call 112 (ambulance 108, maternal and child 102, fire 101, police 100, disaster helpline 1078).
2. Say where you are (landmark or GPS), what happened, how many people, and whether they are awake and breathing.
3. Mention pregnancy, allergies, medical conditions and mobility needs.
4. Stay on the line until told to hang up. Send a SafeHaven SOS to share your location; with no signal, use SMS.

## Using SafeHaven in an emergency
tags: app, safehaven, sos, how to use, sms, offline, alert, share location, map
1. Open the SOS tab, choose the emergency type, answer the questions and follow the steps shown.
2. Send the alert. Your priority level and location go to responders along with the details you chose to share.
3. No network: the alert is queued and can be sent by SMS to your emergency contact. It is delivered automatically when the connection returns.
4. The Map tab ranks shelters by medical staff and supplies. The Respond tab is for responders.
5. This assistant gives guidance only. It is not a doctor and never replaces calling 112.
