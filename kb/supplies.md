## Emergency supply substitutes and field alternatives
tags: supply alternative, out of stock, substitute, no water, no medicine, or substitute, emergency rations, makeshift dressing, clean water
1. Safe drinking water without tablets: Boil water vigorously for at least 1 full minute (3 minutes at high altitude). Let it cool naturally. If boiling is impossible, filter murky water through multiple layers of clean cotton cloth, then use household unscented bleach (2 drops of 6% sodium hypochlorite per 1 litre of clear water; wait 30 minutes before drinking).
2. Homemade Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) substitute: Mix 6 level teaspoons of sugar and 1/2 level teaspoon of salt into 1 litre of clean boiled water. Stir until dissolved. Taste should be no saltier than tears. Give frequent small sips.
3. Sterile wound dressings and gauze: Use clean, unused cotton cloth, t-shirts, or handkerchiefs. If unsterile, iron the cloth with high heat on both sides or scorch briefly over clean heat to kill surface pathogens before applying.
4. Makeshift splints for limb fractures: Use rolled newspapers, magazines, flat wooden rulers, straight tree branches, or cardboard. Pad with towels or cloth and tie firmly above and below the fracture using strips of cloth. Never tie directly over the fracture.
5. Improvised emergency warmth / hypothermia: Layer dry newspapers between clothing for insulation. Wrap person in a large plastic bag or garbage bag with head hole to stop evaporative cooling and wind chill.
6. Sanitary pads and maternal hygiene alternative: Layer clean, soft cotton cloths. Change every 4 to 6 hours. Wash thoroughly with clean water and soap, dry in direct sunlight. Never use unwashed rags, leaves, or dirty plastic against sensitive skin.
7. Baby formula shortage: Exclusively breastfeed if possible (even mothers under stress produce protective milk). For non-breastfed infants over 6 months in acute emergency, clean boiled animal milk diluted with clean boiled water (3 parts milk to 1 part water) with a tiny pinch of sugar can provide short-term survival calories until relief supplies arrive.
