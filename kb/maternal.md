## Labour and imminent childbirth
tags: labour, labor, delivery, childbirth, giving birth, contractions, water broke, baby coming, crowning, pushing
1. Call 112 or 102 and a health worker or ASHA. Go to the nearest facility only if you can travel safely.
2. Birth is near if contractions are 2 to 3 minutes apart, she feels the urge to push, or the baby's head is visible. Then do not travel.
3. Help her lie semi-sitting or on her left side on clean cloths. Wash your hands. Let the baby come out by itself and support the head gently. Never pull.
4. If the cord is loose around the neck, slip it over the head. Do not pull the cord.
5. After birth: dry the baby, wrap warm, place skin to skin on the mother's chest and cover both and the baby's head. Note the time.
6. Do not cut the cord; wait for responders. Let the placenta come out by itself and keep it with the baby.
7. Encourage breastfeeding and gently massage her lower belly. Heavy bleeding needs urgent help.
Call 112 if: always.

## Danger signs in pregnancy
tags: pregnant, pregnancy, eclampsia, preeclampsia, bleeding, headache, blurred vision, reduced movement, water leaking, fever, swelling
Call 112 or 102 or go to the nearest facility at once for any of these:
1. Vaginal bleeding.
2. Fits or convulsions.
3. Severe headache with blurred vision, or sudden swelling of face and hands.
4. Severe belly pain.
5. Fluid leaking, or labour starting before 37 weeks.
6. Baby moving much less or not at all.
7. Fever with chills.
8. Difficulty breathing or chest pain.
9. Vomiting so much she cannot keep fluids down.
While waiting, lie on the left side. Take the MCP card and medicines.

## Heavy bleeding after childbirth
tags: postpartum haemorrhage, pph, bleeding after delivery, placenta, after birth
1. Call 112 or 102 now. Lay her flat, legs raised, and keep her warm.
2. Massage the lower belly firmly in circles until it feels hard like a ball. Keep massaging.
3. Put the baby to the breast; suckling helps the womb contract.
4. Help her pass urine if she can. Press a clean pad on any visible tear.
5. Give nothing by mouth if she is faint or drowsy.

## Bleeding or pain in early pregnancy
tags: miscarriage, spotting, early pregnancy, ectopic, cramps, bleeding
1. Light spotting: rest and see a doctor the same day.
2. Heavy bleeding (soaking a pad in an hour), strong one-sided belly pain, shoulder pain, dizziness or fainting: call 112. It may be an ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage.
3. Put nothing in the vagina. Keep any tissue passed to show the doctor.

## Newborn care in emergencies
tags: newborn, baby, infant, breastfeeding, warm, cord, fever baby
1. Keep the baby warm: skin to skin, head covered, wrapped; never leave on a cold floor.
2. Breastfeed soon after birth and often. Wash hands first. Give no water, honey or other fluids.
3. Danger signs: not feeding, fast or difficult breathing, fever or very cold, fits, floppy, blue lips, yellow skin in the first 24 hours. Get urgent care or call 108.
4. Keep the cord stump clean and dry.

## Periods and menstrual hygiene in emergencies
tags: sanitary pad, menstrual, period, hygiene, shelter, cloth, privacy, menstruation
1. Ask shelter staff or volunteers for pads and for female volunteers. You have a right to privacy and dignity.
2. No pads: use several layers of clean cotton cloth. Change every 4 to 6 hours, wash with soap and clean water, dry fully in sun or air, store clean. Never share cloths.
3. Wrap used pads in a bag and put them in the bin. Wash hands after.
4. Soaking a pad every hour for several hours, fever or foul smell: see a health worker.
5. The SafeHaven Map tab shows which shelters have hygiene kits in stock.

## Medicines and chronic conditions during evacuation
tags: medicine, medication, insulin, chronic, diabetes, bp, hypertension, thyroid, epilepsy, tablets, prescription
1. Carry at least 7 days of regular medicines in original packs with the prescription or a photo of it.
2. Insulin: keep cool and never frozen, for example in a wet cloth or insulated bag.
3. Do not stop treatment for diabetes, blood pressure, epilepsy, TB, HIV or heart disease. Tell the shelter health team what you take.
4. Keep inhaler, auto-injector, spare glasses, hearing aid batteries and mobility aids with you.
5. Complete your SafeHaven profile so responders know what you need.

## Mental health: panic and distress
tags: want to die, suicide, self harm, hopeless, panic, anxiety, fear, trauma, grief, overwhelmed, stress, cannot calm down, crying
1. Slow your breathing: in for 4 counts, out for 6, repeat for a few minutes.
2. Ground yourself: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear.
3. Stay near someone you trust. Drink water, eat something, rest when you can.
4. Tele-MANAS 14416 is a free 24/7 mental health helpline. If you think of harming yourself, call 112 or 14416 now and stay with someone.

## Safety, harassment and violence against women
tags: women safety, harassment, assault, abuse, violence, unsafe, helpline, 181, 1091, rape, threat
1. If you are in danger, call 112. Women's helpline 181; police women's helpline 1091; child helpline 1098.
2. In shelters, ask for a place near other women and families in a lit area. Go to toilets in pairs. Tell the coordinator or police about any threat.
3. Keep your phone charged and send a SafeHaven SOS to share your live location.
4. After a sexual assault: go to a safe place, seek medical care (emergency treatment is available), and know it is not your fault. Do not wash or change clothes if you may want to report.
