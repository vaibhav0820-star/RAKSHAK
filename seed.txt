Q: my father collapsed and is not breathing
Q: someone is unconscious and not responding, what do I do
Q: how do I do CPR
A:
Call 112 now.
1. Lay them on a firm, flat surface. Tilt the head back and check breathing for up to 10 seconds.
2. Not breathing normally: push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, 5-6 cm deep, 100-120 times a minute.
3. If you are trained, give 2 breaths after every 30 pushes. If not, keep pushing only.
4. Use an AED when it arrives and follow its prompts. Do not stop until help takes over.

Q: deep cut and blood is pouring out
Q: she is bleeding heavily from her leg
Q: bleeding will not stop
A:
Call 112 now.
1. Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth and keep pressing. Do not lift it to check.
2. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top.
3. Keep the person lying down and warm. Raise the limb if you do not suspect a fracture.
4. Give nothing to eat or drink. Do not pull out anything stuck in the wound.

Q: my husband is choking on food
Q: she cannot breathe or speak, something is stuck in her throat
A:
Call 112 now if they cannot breathe, speak or cough.
1. Lean them forward and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades.
2. Then give up to 5 abdominal thrusts: fist above the navel, pull sharply inward and upward.
3. Keep alternating until it clears. If they become unresponsive, start CPR.
4. Pregnant or very large person: use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.

Q: my baby is choking
A:
Call 112 now.
1. Lay the baby face down along your forearm with the head lower than the body.
2. Give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades.
3. Turn the baby over and give 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. No abdominal thrusts for babies.
4. Repeat until it clears. If the baby stops responding, start CPR.

Q: I think I am having a heart attack

Q: boiling water spilled on my child's arm
Q: I burned my hand on the stove
A:
1. Cool the burn under clean running water for 20 minutes. No ice, butter, toothpaste or oil.
2. Remove rings or loose clothing near it unless stuck to the skin.
3. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth. Do not burst blisters.
4. Go to hospital or call 112 if it is bigger than the palm, on the face, hands or joints, deep, or in a child.

Q: it is extremely hot and she is confused and not sweating
Q: heatstroke what do I do
A:
Call 112 now. Confusion and hot dry skin point to heatstroke.
1. Move her to shade or a cool room and remove extra clothing.
2. Cool her fast: wet the skin, fan her, and put cold packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
3. If she is awake, give small sips of cool water or ORS.
4. Keep cooling until help arrives.

Q: my diabetic mother is sweating, shaky and confused
A:

2. Recheck after 15 minutes and repeat if she is still low, then give a snack.
3. If she is unconscious or cannot swallow, put nothing in her mouth, place her on her side and call 112.

Q: a snake bit my brother in the field
A:
Call 108 or 112 and get him to a hospital with antivenom now.
1. Keep him calm and still. Keep the bitten limb at heart level and remove rings.

Q: she is in labour and there is no ambulance
Q: the baby's head is visible, what do I do
A:
Call 112 or 102 now.
1. If birth is near, do not travel. Lay her semi-sitting or on her left side on clean cloths.
2. Let the baby come out by itself and support the head gently. Never pull.

4. Do not cut the cord. Let the placenta come out by itself.

Q: how do I keep my newborn warm in the shelter
Q: my newborn is not feeding and feels hot
A:
1. Keep the baby skin to skin on your chest with the head covered, wrapped and off the cold floor.
2. Breastfeed soon and often. Give no water, honey or other fluids.
3. Get urgent care or call 108 if the baby is not feeding, breathes fast, has fever or is very cold, has fits, is floppy, has blue lips, or looks yellow in the first 24 hours.

Q: there are no sanitary pads at the shelter, what can I use
Q: I got my period during the flood
A:
1. Ask the shelter staff or volunteers for pads and for a female volunteer. You have a right to privacy.
2. No pads: use several layers of clean cotton cloth, change every 4 to 6 hours, wash with soap and clean water, dry fully in sun or air.
3. Never share cloths. Wrap used pads in a bag and wash your hands.
4. See a health worker if you soak a pad every hour for several hours or have fever.
5. The SafeHaven Map tab shows shelters with hygiene kits in stock.

Q: what medicines should I take when we evacuate
Q: how do I keep insulin cool without a fridge
A:
1. Take at least 7 days of your regular medicines in original packs with the prescription or a photo of it.
2. Insulin: keep it cool but never frozen, for example in a wet cloth or insulated bag.
3. Do not stop treatment for diabetes, blood pressure, epilepsy, TB, HIV or heart disease. Tell the shelter health team.
4. Keep your inhaler, auto-injector, glasses and mobility aids with you.

Q: water is coming into our house, should we leave
Q: flood is rising what should we do
A:
Leave early for higher ground.
1. Never walk, swim or drive through moving water. Even ankle-deep fast water can knock you down.
2. Take your go-bag, medicines and documents. Send a SafeHaven SOS if anyone needs help.
3. Stay away from fallen cables and switch off mains power only if it is safe and dry.
4. Drink only boiled or chlorinated water.

Q: earthquake right now
A:
1. Drop, cover and hold on under a sturdy table. Stay away from windows.
2. Do not run outside during shaking or use lifts.
3. Outdoors: move to open ground away from buildings and wires.
4. Afterwards expect aftershocks, check for injuries and gas smell, leave damaged buildings by the stairs, and do not light matches.
---
Q: cyclone warning, what should we do
A:
1. Evacuate early to a cyclone shelter on higher ground when told to. Do not wait for the storm.
2. Take your go-bag, medicines, documents, power bank and water.
3. Stay indoors away from windows. The calm eye is followed by strong winds again.
4. Afterwards avoid fallen wires and floodwater.

Q: there is a fire in the building

Leave now and call 101 or 112.

2. Stay low under the smoke and cover your mouth with a wet cloth.

Q: what do I tell the ambulance when I call
A:
1. Call 112 (ambulance 108, maternal and child 102, fire 101, police 100).
2. Say where you are (landmark or GPS), what happened, how many people, and whether they are awake and breathing.
3. Mention pregnancy, allergies, medical conditions and mobility needs.
4. Stay on the line until told to hang up.
